# Code of conduct

## Our pledge

We want LeakStop to be a place where anyone can report a bug, suggest a change or send a patch without being treated badly. We pledge to make taking part in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, whatever their age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity and expression, level of experience, nationality, appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Our standards

Behaviour that helps:

- being kind and patient, especially with people who are new,
- respecting other points of view and experiences,
- giving and accepting criticism of the work, not of the person,
- owning mistakes, apologising, and learning from them,
- keeping the discussion on what is best for the project and its users.

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- sexualised language or images, and unwelcome attention of any kind,
- trolling, insults, and personal or political attacks,
- harassment, in public or in private,
- publishing someone else's private information, such as an address or an email, without their permission,
- anything else that would reasonably be seen as inappropriate in a professional setting.

A real secret pasted into an issue is also a problem, not only of manners: please use a fake one, and rotate the real one.

## Enforcement responsibilities

The maintainer is responsible for explaining what is acceptable and will act, fairly and when needed, in response to behaviour they think is inappropriate. That can mean removing, editing or rejecting comments, commits, issues and other contributions, and explaining why.

## Scope

This applies in every space of the project (the repository, issues, pull requests and discussions) and when someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report abuse or unacceptable behaviour by email to **alexandre.martiol@gmail.com**. Reports are handled in private, and the person who reports is protected from retaliation. For a security problem use the private route in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead.

## Consequences

The maintainer will choose the response they think fits, going from a private note asking to stop, to a warning, to a temporary ban from the project, to a permanent ban for a pattern of bad behaviour or for harassment.
